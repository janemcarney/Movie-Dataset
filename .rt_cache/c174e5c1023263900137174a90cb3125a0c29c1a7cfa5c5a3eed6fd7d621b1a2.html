Critics Consensus

Clint Eastwood's assured direction - combined with knockout performances from Hilary Swank and Morgan Freeman - help Million Dollar Baby to transcend its clichés, and the result is deeply heartfelt and moving.

Chris S @Janomi 1d Million Dollar Baby has plenty to like, especially Hilary Swank’s performance as Maggie Fitzgerald. She gives Maggie toughness, hunger, innocence, and heartbreak without making her feel false. Maggie is not chasing fame as much as purpose, and Swank makes you feel how badly she needs boxing to give her life meaning. Clint Eastwood’s restrained direction gives the film a quiet, bruised feeling. The gym, lighting, silences, and slower pacing create a lonely atmosphere that fits the story. The bond between Frankie and Maggie is the emotional center, slowly growing from a reluctant trainer-fighter partnership into something deeper and more parental. Morgan Freeman brings warmth and sadness as Eddie, giving the gym history and softening some of Frankie’s harshness. The final act is powerful but extremely heavy, shifting the movie into much darker territory. Maggie’s family is also written too broadly. There is plenty to admire, but the emotional weight can feel punishing.
